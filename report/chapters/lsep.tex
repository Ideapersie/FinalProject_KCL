\chapter{Legal, Social and Ethical Considerations}
\label{ch:lsep}

\section{Patient Safety and Clinical Liability}
\label{sec:lsep-safety}

The asymmetry of harm in clinical question answering, where an unnecessary
retrieval costs seconds; a confidently wrong drug-interaction answer could
cost a patient, is not a rhetorical framing in this project but an
\emph{encoded design decision}: the gate ensemble is deliberately biased
toward retrieving when its signals disagree
(Section~\ref{sec:method-why-three}), and the risk tagger's precedence rules
default toward the more cautious label. Safety asymmetry shaped the
artefact, not just the write-up.

The evaluation then returned a result that this chapter must state as
plainly as the technical chapters do: the safety envelope of the reference
system is empty. At 3B no policy clears even the lowest stipulated accuracy
bar; the larger models of Chapter~\ref{ch:scaling} clear the low-risk bar
only, and none approaches the medium-risk bar. Therefore \textbf{this
system must not be used for clinical decision support}, in
education-adjacent settings included, without qualified human oversight. A dissertation that quantifies its own system's
unfitness for deployment is making the safety-relevant contribution;
concealing that distance would be the ethical failure.

Liability follows the same logic. If such a system were deployed and gave a
harmful answer, responsibility would distribute contentiously across the
model provider, the deployer, and the clinician who acted on the output,
and regulatory regimes increasingly refuse to let the software disclaim its
way out. Under the UK MHRA's software-as-a-medical-device guidance and the
EU AI Act's high-risk classification for clinical decision support, a
deployed version of this system would attract conformity obligations this
research prototype has never been assessed against. It is a benchmark
instrument, not a device, and the report says so.

One empirical finding sharpens the automation-bias concern beyond the
generic. Section~\ref{sec:eval-distraction} showed that when retrieval
misleads the model, its wrong answers become \emph{longer and more fluent}
($15\times$ longer than the terse correct closed-book answers). Fluency and
confidence are exactly the surface features that induce human over-trust.
The failure mode this system exhibits is therefore the \emph{most} dangerous
kind for a clinical reader, namely wrong answers that look thorough, which is
an independent argument for surfacing machine uncertainty
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-deployment}) rather than only polished prose.

\section{Privacy, Data Governance and Sovereignty}
\label{sec:lsep-privacy}

The offline, CPU-only architecture is a \emph{privacy architecture}, not
merely a cost profile. No query, no patient context, and no retrieved
passage ever leaves the device: there is no API call to log, no third-party
processor to contract with, and no cross-border transfer to assess. That is
what makes the design viable precisely where GDPR, NHS data-governance
frameworks and analogous regimes make cloud LLM use difficult or
impermissible, and it is why ``offline'' appears in the problem statement
rather than the limitations.

Honesty about what this project did \emph{not} have to handle belongs here.
The corpus is built from public sources (medical textbooks and PubMed
abstracts) and the benchmarks are public research datasets; \textbf{no
patient data was processed at any point}. The credentialed EHR datasets
contemplated in the original project plan were not used. The privacy
property of the architecture is real, but it was demonstrated on public
data; claiming hard-won privacy engineering would be false, and the accurate
claim is architectural: a system with no network egress cannot leak what it
processes.

Data licensing was respected in the corpus build: MedRAG's corpora are
distributed for research use, and the derived indexes remain local research
artefacts, not redistributed products.

\section{Bias, Equity and Access}
\label{sec:lsep-bias}

Neither of the gate's two paths is neutral. When the system retrieves, it
inherits the perspective of its corpus, namely Western-published textbooks and
PubMed abstracts, whose epidemiological assumptions, drug availability and
demographic baselines are baked into every passage. When the gate skips, the
system inherits the training distribution of the base model instead. A gate
therefore does not remove bias; it \emph{selects between two bias profiles}
per query, and no analysis in this study measures the demographic
consequences of that selection, which is an acknowledged gap.

The equity argument that motivates the project also carries its own warning.
An offline CPU-only system is exactly the configuration that can run where
GPUs, budgets and connectivity do not exist, such as rural practice, LMIC
clinics, training hospitals, the settings where only 14\% of rural
residents are online at all \citep{itu2025factsfigures}. Those settings have the most to gain from
deployable clinical QA, and the least infrastructure to verify it. But
Section~\ref{sec:eval-envelope} shows the present system does not meet even
lenient safety bars, and shipping an unsafe system to under-resourced
settings, where clinical backstop capacity is thinnest, would be worse
than shipping nothing. Equity of access must mean access to a \emph{safe}
system; this work quantifies how far that still is.

\section{Environmental Cost}
\label{sec:lsep-energy}

Per-query energy was measured throughout with \texttt{codecarbon} and is a
software estimate, not a metered hardware measurement, and is reported as such.
The honest accounting cuts against the intuitive green claim: the gate
halves retrieval calls but \emph{adds} three draft generations per query, so
on this hardware, where generation dominates energy, gating is not
currently an energy win (Section~\ref{sec:eval-cost}). The energy case for
gating is real but conditional: it holds where retrieval dominates the cost
profile (large corpora, remote or disk-bound indexes), not on this laptop.
No unearned environmental benefit is claimed; the merged-draft optimisation
of Section~\ref{sec:conc-future} is the identified path to reducing the
gate's own footprint. More broadly, the project's choice of a small
quantised model on commodity hardware sits at the frugal end of the field's
energy spectrum \citep{strubell2019}, which is itself an environmentally
relevant design position.
