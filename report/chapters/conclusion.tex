\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conc-summary}

This dissertation asked when an offline, CPU-only medical question-answering
system should retrieve, and answered it with a training-free three-gate
ensemble evaluated end to end on three quantised models over a 426K-chunk
medical corpus. Seven contributions follow, each carrying its evidence.

\textbf{C1, a working training-free gate.} Token entropy, logit margin and a
two-draft hallucination probe combined by majority vote with hardware-aware
degradation, requiring no fine-tuning, no labelled data and no second model
(Chapter~\ref{ch:method}).

\textbf{C2, calibration non-transfer.} Literature thresholds are physically
unreachable on a quantised model, and offline replay recalibration finds
usable operating points in seconds, so gate thresholds are model-specific and
must be treated as such (Section~\ref{sec:eval-calibration}).

\textbf{C3, retrieval as distraction.} Retrieval broke \breakLlama{}
previously correct answers and fixed \fixLlama{}, with chunks equally on-topic
but $2.4\times$ less answer-bearing where it harmed
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-distraction}).

\textbf{C4, the protective skip.} On those poisoned questions the gate's skip
decision scored 19/19 against 3/27 for its retrieve decision, so its value is
protection at the boundary of parametric knowledge rather than cost saving
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-protective}).

\textbf{C5, the safety envelope.} A risk-stratified measurement of the
distance between this system and clinical acceptability, empty at 3B and
clearing only the low-risk bar at 7B and 14B
(Sections~\ref{sec:eval-envelope}, \ref{sec:scaling-bars}).

\textbf{C6, a three-model scaling study.} Identical corpus, questions and
code at 3B, 7B and 14B, showing a closed-book curve that plateaus below the
medium-risk bar, calibration non-transfer recurring prospectively at every
scale, distraction growing with capability, and a selective-retrieval
advantage that reaches significance at 14B (Chapter~\ref{ch:scaling}).

\textbf{C7, a reproducible harness.} Log-generated numbers behind a
drift-guard test, 240 model-free tests, paired-run proofs and verified
determinism (Chapter~\ref{ch:impl}).

Every claim is held at the strength its evidence supports, so the retrieval
halving (\retPfiveMcq{} against \retPoneMcq{}) is exact, the 3B accuracy gain
is consistent with an improvement while the 14B gain
(\diffFourteenPfivePone{}pp, CI \diffFourteenPfivePoneCI{}) is established,
and the medium-risk bar is unreached at every scale. If one sentence survives
this dissertation it should be that retrieval, the field's default remedy for
hallucination, made these models worse on questions they already knew, most of
all the largest, and the gate's contribution is knowing when not to use it.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Extending the scaling curve.} A second model family at 7B to
    14B would separate family effects from scale effects, a fourth scale
    would test whether the closed-book plateau is genuine saturation, and an
    ensemble whose every member is recalibratable would restore the
    matched-budget comparison this study could not make
    (Section~\ref{sec:scaling-budget}).
  \item \textbf{Content-aware entropy.} Restricting the entropy mean to
    content tokens, or weighting it by attention to the question's clinical
    terms, attacks the method's clearest measured weakness
    (Section~\ref{sec:eval-attribution}) and can be prototyped offline against
    the logged per-token arrays.
  \item \textbf{Retrieval-aware gating.} The current gate must commit before
    seeing any evidence, so a second-stage gate asking whether the retrieved
    chunks are actually decisive could reject a retrieval after the fact and
    reclaim part of the 3/27 failure region.
  \item \textbf{Gate cost optimisation.} Merging the entropy and margin
    drafts cuts five model calls per query to four, and a probe-free
    logit-only configuration would remove the two probe drafts that dominate
    cost, at an accuracy trade-off still to be quantified
    (Section~\ref{sec:eval-cost}).
  \item \textbf{Statistical power.} The full 7,663-question MIRAGE benchmark,
    multiple seeds and a deliberately balanced high-risk stratum would
    convert suggestive intervals into decidable ones.
  \item \textbf{Deployment surface and attribution.} Extending the interface of
    Section~\ref{sec:impl-demo} into a validated clinician-facing review tool,
    physically benchmarking the low and high hardware tiers on real 4\,GB and
    16\,GB devices, and scoring the implemented-but-unevaluated citation policy
    (P2) for grounded precision and recall against an ALCE-style attribution
    corpus.
\end{enumerate}

The through-line of all six is the dissertation's own, which is that in
safety-critical and resource-constrained settings the interesting question is
not how to make a model retrieve better but how to make a system know when its
model already knows.
